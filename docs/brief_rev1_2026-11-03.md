## 2. The design the sections must state

This section is a summary for you. Write the paper from it and from the sources in §1; do not paste it.

### 2.1 Thesis, research questions, case study

**Thesis.** "The explainer assumes stationarity where the model does not." Display it in the Introduction, as v5.1 §1.1 places it.

**Research questions**
- **RQ1:** How large and how structured is the distortion that unconditional backgrounds (B0, B1, B-pristine) introduce into temporal Shapley attributions of models of mean-nonstationary data?
- **RQ2:** Do trend-aware backgrounds reduce that distortion? How much of the prediction do they still explain, and how do they compare with a learned conditional imputer (B4)?
- **RQ3:** Do the distortion signatures found on models of known functions also appear on RUL models trained on generated units, and on models trained on the measured cells of the same datasets?

**Case study.** The re-examination of the ACIIDS findings is a scoped, two-step case study (v5.1 §3.14), not a research question.

### 2.2 Contributions (draft for the Introduction; the authors will edit them)

1. A taxonomy of substitution artifacts (A1–A6) with one instrument per artifact, measured against exact ground truth.
2. TAB-state and TAB-traj, defined formally for general mean-nonstationarity, each with its assumptions, diagnostics and operating envelope.
3. An evaluation design for masking backgrounds.
   - A trained model reproduces a known function on its data support. The ground truth is therefore exact for every background, and the distortion a background causes is separated from the information it removes.
   - The design is built on DegradX.
4. A two-step case study of the authors' published attribution findings, on a corrected pipeline.

**Do not claim a benchmark or testbed as a contribution.** That contribution is DegradX.

### 2.3 Each background asks its own question

Shapley values sum to v(N) − v(∅), so the background fixes what is explained.

| Background | All cells masked → f evaluated on | Attributions sum to | Question answered |
|---|---|---|---|
| B0, B1, B-pristine (unconditional) | a constant window | f(x) − f(b) | Why does this prediction differ from a fixed reference? |
| B2 TAB-state (coalition-independent, fine bins) | the population at this window's states | f(x) − f(b_state(x)) | Why does it differ from a typical unit at the same state? |
| B3 TAB-traj (coalition-independent) | the unit's own extrapolated trajectory | f(x) − f(b_traj(x)) | What do the observations add beyond the unit's own trajectory? |
| B2c, B4 (depend on the coalition) | the population marginal | ≈ f(x) − E f | The B1 question, with coalitions that stay on the data support |

The paper must:
- state the explained quantity for every background where that background is defined;
- report the explained mass beside the distortion wherever distortion is reported.

### 2.4 Ground truth per background, and distortion

**M-obs models**
- Train on y_obs := Σ_{(u,c)∈W} w_{u,c}(x_{u,c} − x⁰_c). This is DegradX's reference model g applied to the observed window.
- A function-identity gate checks that every member reproduces g on held-out generated windows, to within a declared fraction of the SD of Σ w ε (threshold: D8).

**Ground truth for background b, ϕ\*_b**
- It is the Shapley values of the known function (g, or ψ∘g for M-ψ) under b's value function.
- For a coalition-independent b and affine g, it is w ⊙ (x − b) in closed form.
- Otherwise, compute it by permutation sampling on the analytic function, to a declared precision.

**Distortion: D_b := A_b(f) − ϕ\*_b**
- It is the part of the attribution caused by the model's behaviour off its data support, separated from the information loss ϕ\*_b.
- This separates the two parts the thesis names in v5.1 §1.1.
- TAB is not correct by construction: it passes only if its coalitions keep f on its support.

**Ensemble divergence**
- The spread of A_b across the ten members, which agree on the support.
- It measures dependence on unconstrained behaviour without needing ground truth, and it runs unchanged on measured data.

**Explained mass**
- |f(x) − f(b)| for coalition-independent backgrounds, and |v(N) − v(∅)| otherwise.

### 2.5 DegradX, and the extensions this paper declares

**Used as it is**
- the MATR, HUST and FORM profiles;
- the generator and seeds;
- the exact decomposition of every unit (z, m, p, ε, and m_state = φ_c(z) without offsets);
- the reference model g;
- the null channels and their gates;
- the 10-member LSTM ensembles (configurations 64×2 and 128×1, five seeds each);
- the scores (rank agreement, channel allocation error, temporal profile error);
- the TSTR fidelity of each profile;
- the 120 evaluation windows of each profile.

**Extensions declared in this paper**
- **Two target variables.**
  - y_obs, for M-obs.
  - ψ(y_obs), for M-ψ, with a declared monotone nonlinear ψ. Whether M-ψ is run is decision D5.
- **A trended null channel.**
  - It applies a monotone channel mapping to the degradation-state trajectory of another unit, at a random phase, and has zero target weight.
  - It is accepted only if it passes DegradX's null-leakage gate and its conditional channel-use check.
- **An event-driven target on FORM, for A3.**
  - It is the sparse part of DegradX's target alone, Σ w p.
  - Its ground truth on windows without patterns is zero everywhere.
- **Stress variants** made with DegradX's existing generator overrides (transition sharpness, noise variance, pattern amplitude). Label them as not calibrated.
- **One longer window length,** for A5 and the within-window view (decision D2).

**What DegradX's own results imply.** State each point in the Methodology where it motivates a choice, cite DegradX for it, and take the numbers from the DegradX paper.
- **Equally accurate models disagree.** Ten such models differ more than attribution methods do, so scores against ϕ\* on models of y cannot rank backgrounds. Hence the per-background ground truth of §2.4.
- **Zero-weight channels that carry the state are read by models.** This is shown on MATR. Trap channels must therefore be independent of the unit's state.
- **FORM's inserted patterns carry a negligible share of y.** A3 therefore needs the event-driven target.

### 2.6 Backgrounds

| ID | Background | Status | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| B0 | Raw zeros | Floor | Attributions are computed in raw input space. Zero after standardization equals the training mean, which is B1 |
| B1 | Per-channel training mean (D3) | Incumbent; the ACIIDS background | |
| B-pristine | x⁰_c = φ_c(0) at every position | Incumbent; DegradX's primary baseline | On measured data, x⁰ comes from the paired profile |
| B2 | TAB-state | Proposed | State index computed from x_{1:t−1} |
| B3 | TAB-traj | Proposed | Fit on the strict past; falls back to B2 below t_min |

### 2.8 Models, estimator, data, statistics

**Synthetic models** (the DegradX LSTM, decision D7)
- M-obs and M-ψ.
- M-y: the released DegradX ensembles, read only with instruments that need no ground truth.
- M-R: target R; inputs are the channels plus elapsed position, and elapsed position is unmasked context, not a player.
- A GBT on flattened windows, as the non-recurrent control for A5.

**Estimator**
- TimeSHAP at DegradX's declared settings: cell level, nsamples := 32,000, pruning off.
- The background is passed as a sequence of the window's length. TimeSHAP 1.0.4 accepts this.
- Backgrounds that depend on the coalition (B2c, B4) need a modified masking step in the kernel. State this.
- The cross-estimator check is as in v5.1 §3.6.

**Measured data**
- The MATR, HUST and FORM cells, each paired with its profile. Report each profile's TSTR ratio as its fidelity.
- NASA PCoE is used only for the case study: the corrected ACIIDS pipeline, with the attention LSTM.

**Statistics**
- Paired contrasts within a model, on the same windows and with the same sampling seed.
- Bootstrap over units, as in DegradX.
- The number of ensemble members that agree in sign.
- Stratification by the exact z on generated data, and by the state index on measured data.
